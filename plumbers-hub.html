Core Standards

6 Installation Essentials

Proven site guidelines to ensure structural stability, prevent hydraulic strain, and maintain leak-free water storage.

1. Firm, Level Base

A reinforced concrete slab is strongly preferred. Never install directly onto bare soil, soft backfill, or loose stones that settle unevenly when wet.

2. Full Bottom Support

The entire tank floor must rest flat. Ensure no overhang over plinth edges or between steel tower joists; overhang creates sharp rim stresses that deform polyethylene.

3. Clear of Debris

Always sweep the plinth clean before lowering the tank. Even small sharp stones or cement lumps can puncture or pit the base under the immense weight of stored water.

4. Clear of Sharp or Hot Surfaces

Maintain safe clearance from jagged corrugated roofing edges, generator exhaust flues, incinerators, and open flames. Polyethylene must not be exposed to concentrated heat.

5. Flexible Connectors at Outlets

Fit a flexible connection, rubber bellow, or looped section immediately after the gate valve. This absorbs natural thermal expansion and settling without snapping rigid pipe fittings.

6. Check Fittings Before Filling

Hand-tighten brass and polyethylene threaded fittings plus a quarter turn using PTFE thread seal tape. Never over-torque or cross-thread moulded polyethylene BSP sockets.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Plumbing & Installation Questions

Quick answers to real-world questions frequently encountered by plumbers and fundis on site.

Can I place a tank directly on bare ground?

No. Placing a tank on bare ground is never recommended. When rainwater collects around the site, uncompacted soil softens, leading to uneven ground subsidence. This causes the tank to tilt and subjects the lower base to localized high-pressure points. Even a slight slope creates asymmetric hydraulic pressure on the walls. Always cast a level reinforced concrete plinth over compacted hardcore.

Why is my tank leaking around the brass outlet fitting?

The two most frequent causes of outlet leaks are unsupported pipe weight and over-tightened threads:

  • Unsupported pipe weight: If heavy metal pipes or gate valves hang freely without wall brackets, their leverage pulls down on the tank socket, distorting the seal.
  • Over-tightening: Over-torquing brass fittings into polyethylene can strip the internal plastic threads or crack the moulded boss. Use quality PTFE tape, tighten hand-tight plus 1/4 to 1/2 turn with a wrench, and install a flexible coupling.
Do I need a vent pipe on my KENTANK installation?

Yes, adequate venting is essential. When water is rapidly pumped or drawn from a tank, air must enter freely to replace the displaced volume. If the lid is screwed down airtight without an open overflow or screened vent, rapid discharge can create a vacuum powerful enough to implode or buckle the tank walls. Similarly, fast inflow must allow displaced air to escape without pressurizing the tank.

What size overflow pipe should I install?

Your overflow pipe diameter must always be equal to or greater than the largest inflow pipe. For rainwater harvesting systems where gutter downpipes are 3-inch (75mm) or 4-inch (110mm), an undersized 1-inch or 1.5-inch overflow cannot discharge torrential tropical rainfall quickly enough. Water will back up and force its way through the manhole rim. Always fit an insect screen on the discharge end of the overflow.
